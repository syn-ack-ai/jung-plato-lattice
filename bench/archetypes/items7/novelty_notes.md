# items7 novelty notes (README addendum 36, 2026-11-01)

Written by the pattern inventor (Claude) after drafting `assign7.py`. Every myth pattern was checked against the
question "is this the plot of a known myth, legend, fable, folk tale, fairy tale, scripture story, epic, famous novel,
film or well-known parable?". The check used only the inventor's own knowledge. No web or corpus search was done (see
"Limits" at the end).

Final counts: 80 myth patterns, 80 mechanism patterns, 160 unique ids, hints 27 to 39 words.

## Kept, with a distant echo noted (judged not the plot of the named story)

- bell_wall: the vow echoes "swords into ploughshares" (Isaiah 2:4). That is a phrase, not a plot. The bell wall is
  new.
- taxed_laughter: the never-laughing princess tales and Timm Thaler (a sold laugh). Here a tax is the cause and the
  child never learns to laugh. Different causal step.
- singing_gold: sirens and Tolkien's dwarves who "delved too deep". A lure leading downward away from a loved one,
  with no monster and no shipwreck.
- imitated_thrush: Andersen's The Nightingale. There a fake bird replaces the real one and the emperor prefers it.
  Here the imitator silences the bird and must then sing in its place forever. The bird was changed to a thrush.
- ring_in_bread: the motif of a ring found in a fish or a cake (Polycrates). Here the accuser hides her own false
  accusation.
- sea_returns_boat: the literal-wish motif (The Monkey's Paw). The plot is different.
- current_daughter, milestone_courier: punishing or merging transformations of Ovid's kind. Neither is the plot of a
  specific metamorphosis.
- told_bees: the folk custom of "telling the bees". A custom, not a story.
- moon_debt: fool tales about the moon in a well. Here a debt is paid in a moon that never falls due.
- silent_field: helpfulness tests such as Mother Holle. Here the stated rule (silence) hides the real test.
- canyon_echo: Echo and Narcissus. Different step: a possessive trained echo isolates its owner.
- river_purchase: Midas. Here the payment, not the touch, turns to water, after a boast.
- grandfather_oak: Erysichthon. The tree is not sacred, there is no hunger curse, and the loss is practical.

## Mechanism drafts replaced (not required, recorded for completeness)

- Removed as instances of named effects: road salt kills a hedge, causing more salting (a feedback loop); predator
  removal letting prey boom (a trophic cascade; two drafts); frost heave under an ice rink; a dredged channel making
  well water brackish (saltwater intrusion); hydropeaking at a dam.
- Removed as real, famous incidents: a curved tower melting cars (London's "Walkie-Talkie"); red honey from bees
  eating candy waste (Ribeauville 2012).
- Removed as repeats of another mechanism's key step: rail vibration cracking glass (tram_cheese); a ferry timetable
  (dropped_flight); solvent fumes damaging the neighbor's goods (ammonia_brass); radio-tag gates upsetting a fridge
  (radio_clocks: interference); a thermostat freezing pipes (datacenter_switch); a hedge loss letting topsoil blow
  away (salted_hedge); silt smothering oysters (pen_shade); a marathon delaying deliveries (match_shift); gym
  vibration at the dentist (tram_cheese); church attendance and a pool losing patrons (laptop_exams). Each was
  rewritten with a different causal step.

## Limits

The novelty check relies on one model's memory. Folk-tale corpora (Aarne-Thompson-Uther types, Thompson's motif
index) contain thousands of tale types, so an obscure regional tale could still match a pattern. Before these items
are used, run an independent check: ask 2-3 models from other families "Is this the plot of any known story? Name
it." for each myth hint, and replace any pattern that more than one model names with the same specific source.
